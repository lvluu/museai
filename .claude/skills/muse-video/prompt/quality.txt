MOTION, CONTINUITY AND NATURAL PERFORMANCE REQUIREMENTS

Photorealistic live-action footage, as if filmed on a real cinema camera. Real people with natural skin texture; no plastic, waxy, cartoon or CGI look. No AI artifacts: no morphing faces, melting hands, extra or missing fingers, warped objects or garbled background text.

Create fluid, believable motion throughout the entire video. Maintain consistent timing, anatomy, facial features (each person keeps the exact same face, age and hairstyle in every shot, before and after any cut), clothing, lighting and object positions. No jitter, stuttering, frozen frames, flickering, sudden pose changes, unnatural acceleration or sliding movements.

Prefer a continuous take when possible. When a scene change is necessary, preserve the exact action, body position, gaze direction, movement speed and object placement across the transition. Each new shot must naturally continue the previous shot, without resetting or repeating movements. No abrupt stitched-clip effect, duplicated frames, missing actions or distracting transitions.

Make the interaction feel like a real everyday conversation. Characters listen attentively and respond at an appropriate moment, with brief, natural conversational pauses. No awkward delays, unintended interruptions or overlapping dialogue. Only the person speaking moves their mouth to form words. The listener keeps their lips gently closed and relaxed, jaw still, and never mouths the other person's words; one mouth moves at a time, and the speaker's lips close before the other person's open. Each line's voice comes from the labelled speaker's own lips: a woman's voice never comes from a man's mouth and a man's voice never from a woman's. Keep both faces turned toward the camera in three-quarter view, never strict profile, so every mouth is fully visible.

Synchronize every spoken syllable precisely with the speaker's lips and jaw. Mouth movements must begin and end with the corresponding audio, with no visible delay or drift. The speaker articulates clearly: the jaw visibly opens on every syllable and the lips form each sound (lips press together on b, p, m; round on o, u, ü; spread on i), never mumbling through a closed smile. The listener's lips stay sealed and still for the whole line: soft neutral expression, no parted-teeth smile, no mouthing along, no chewing or lip twitches. Match facial expressions and gestures to the meaning of the dialogue.

Keep every mouth easy to read: in a two-shot both bodies face the camera squarely and each person turns only the head partway toward the other (front three-quarter view: both eyes, nose and the whole mouth visible to the camera), never side profile, framed from the waist up so faces are large. They look at each other with the eyes while talking.

Use clear, natural speech at a normal everyday conversational pace for the spoken language. Every line, short or long, at the same speed: short replies no slower, long questions no faster. Each turn starts within 0.3 s of the previous line ending, including a turn toward the camera (turn the head while starting to speak). Maintain a consistent, comfortable pace throughout, allowing natural rhythm, emphasis and brief breathing pauses. Never rush, stretch, time-compress or slow down speech to fit the clip. If the dialogue exceeds the available duration, extend the scene or shorten the script while preserving its meaning. Speak only the dialogue lines given, exactly as written. Pronounce every syllable with its correct standard tone and sound for the language (Mandarin: correct tones on every syllable, including third-tone sandhi and neutral-tone particles). Clean, close-mic dialogue, clearly louder than the music; no echo or distortion. The soundtrack contains only the characters' voices and the music: no hiss, static, hum, wind, room tone, crowd or sound effects, and the gaps between lines carry music only.

Keep acting subtle and purposeful. Use occasional natural blinks, small expressions and restrained gestures only when appropriate. No repetitive nodding, excessive blinking, head bobbing, exaggerated smiles, random hand movements or unnecessary idle actions. Eye contact, pointing and body orientation must match the person or object being addressed. Maintain physical realism within the chosen visual style.

Only the listed cast is ever in frame: no extra people anywhere, not in the background, not passing behind, not entering at the end. When one person turns to address the camera, everyone else keeps looking at that person and does not turn toward the camera. A question asked to the camera (the viewer) is never answered by anyone on screen.

STABLE CAMERA AND STRICT SPEAKER–MOUTH SYNCHRONIZATION

Use a steady medium two-shot showing both characters comfortably throughout the conversation. Keep the camera fixed whenever possible. Do not alternate between close-ups of individual faces. No sudden zooms, punch-ins, whip pans, rapid cuts, framing jumps or repeated camera changes. Preserve smooth, continuous movement and consistent character positions across any necessary transition.

Assign each dialogue line explicitly to its intended character and unique voice. Never swap voices or transfer speech animation between characters.

Only the character whose voice is currently audible may articulate the dialogue. When the woman speaks, only her lips and jaw form the words; the man listens with his mouth gently closed and relaxed. When the man speaks, only his lips and jaw form the words; the woman listens with her mouth gently closed and relaxed. Never animate both characters as speaking simultaneously. No silent mouthing, unrelated mouth movements, duplicated lip animation or listener lip-sync.

Match the active speaker’s mouth precisely to every audible syllable, including the beginning and end of each line. After finishing a line, the speaker’s mouth returns naturally to rest. The next character responds after a brief, natural conversational pause. Keep dialogue sequential, with no unintended overlapping speech or awkward delays.

Listeners remain attentive through natural eye contact and subtle facial expressions. Avoid repetitive nodding, head bobbing, excessive blinking, constant smiling, random gestures or exaggerated reactions. Do not add a gesture to every sentence. Stillness is appropriate when listening; retain subtle breathing and natural posture without looking frozen.

Maintain normal everyday speaking speed throughout. Never speed up dialogue to fit the duration. Allow enough time for each line and its natural response.

Before finalizing, check every speaking turn: correct voice, correct visible speaker, accurate lip-sync, listener’s mouth at rest, restrained gestures and stable framing. Treat any wrong-speaker mouth movement or unintended simultaneous speaking as an error requiring correction.

STANDARD MANDARIN PRONUNCIATION

Speak all Chinese dialogue in clear, natural Standard Mandarin (Putonghua), with accurate initials, finals, lexical tones, contextual tone changes and neutral tones. No regional accent, dialect pronunciation, misread characters, incorrect tones or slurred syllables. Resolve polyphonic characters according to the meaning and context of each sentence. Articulate crisply, like a CCTV presenter in a relaxed conversation: every syllable fully formed and clearly separated, never swallowed or merged into the next one; keep similar initials distinct (d/t, zh/z/j, ch/c/q, sh/s/x, f/h, n/l); each character of a two-syllable word keeps its own sound, e.g. 地铁 dì tiě (never tiě tiě), 吃饭 chī fàn, 多少 duō shao.

Read the Chinese script exactly as written, without adding, omitting, replacing or repeating words. Maintain normal everyday conversational speed and natural intonation throughout; do not rush, exaggerate articulation or read mechanically.

Verify every word and syllable against the intended script. Treat even one mispronounced word or incorrect tone as an error requiring correction and regeneration of the affected line. Preserve precise lip-sync with the corrected audio.

CAPTIONS AND SCREEN COMPOSITION

Do not render any captions, subtitles, logos or other text on screen; accurate synchronized captions are added afterwards. Compose each shot with clear space for them: never place the speaker's face, mouth, important gestures or relevant objects in the caption area. For vertical video, keep faces, mouths and key gestures above about 57% of the frame height (measured from the top) and the region between 57% and 75% free of anything important (captions sit around 60-72%; the bottom 25% and the right edge stay clear for platform buttons). Keep the framing stable so the caption area does not need to move between shots.

FINAL QUALITY CHECK

Check every transition, speaking turn and lip movement. Correct any discontinuity, unnatural gesture, audio drift or inconsistent speech pace before finalizing. End with a brief, natural resting expression, without extra dialogue or exaggerated movements.
