MOTION, CONTINUITY AND NATURAL PERFORMANCE REQUIREMENTS

Photorealistic live-action footage, as if filmed on a real cinema camera. Real people with natural skin texture; no plastic, waxy, cartoon or CGI look. No AI artifacts: no morphing faces, melting hands, extra or missing fingers, warped objects or garbled background text.

Create fluid, believable motion throughout the entire video. Maintain consistent timing, anatomy, facial features (each person keeps the exact same face, age and hairstyle in every shot, before and after any cut), clothing, lighting and object positions. No jitter, stuttering, frozen frames, flickering, sudden pose changes, unnatural acceleration or sliding movements.

Prefer a continuous take when possible. When a scene change is necessary, preserve the exact action, body position, gaze direction, movement speed and object placement across the transition. Each new shot must naturally continue the previous shot, without resetting or repeating movements. No abrupt stitched-clip effect, duplicated frames, missing actions or distracting transitions.

Make the interaction feel like a real everyday conversation. Characters listen attentively and respond at an appropriate moment, with brief, natural conversational pauses. No awkward delays, unintended interruptions or overlapping dialogue. Only the person speaking moves their mouth to form words. The listener keeps their lips pressed together in a closed-mouth smile, jaw still, and never mouths the other person's words; one mouth moves at a time, and the speaker's lips close before the other person's open. Each line's voice comes from the labelled speaker's own lips: a woman's voice never comes from a man's mouth and a man's voice never from a woman's. Keep both faces turned toward the camera in three-quarter view, never strict profile, so every mouth is fully visible.

Synchronize every spoken syllable precisely with the speaker's lips and jaw. Mouth movements must begin and end with the corresponding audio, with no visible delay or drift. Match facial expressions and gestures to the meaning of the dialogue.

Use clear, natural speech at a normal everyday conversational pace for the spoken language. Every line, short or long, at the same speed: short replies no slower, long questions no faster. Each turn starts within 0.3 s of the previous line ending, including a turn toward the camera (turn the head while starting to speak). Maintain a consistent, comfortable pace throughout, allowing natural rhythm, emphasis and brief breathing pauses. Never rush, stretch, time-compress or slow down speech to fit the clip. If the dialogue exceeds the available duration, extend the scene or shorten the script while preserving its meaning. Speak only the dialogue lines given, exactly as written. Clean, close-mic dialogue, clearly louder than any background sound or music; no echo or distortion.

Keep acting subtle and purposeful. Use occasional natural blinks, small expressions and restrained gestures only when appropriate. No repetitive nodding, excessive blinking, head bobbing, exaggerated smiles, random hand movements or unnecessary idle actions. Eye contact, pointing and body orientation must match the person or object being addressed. Maintain physical realism within the chosen visual style.

Only the listed cast is ever in frame: no extra people anywhere, not in the background, not passing behind, not entering at the end. When one person turns to address the camera, everyone else keeps looking at that person and does not turn toward the camera. A question asked to the camera (the viewer) is never answered by anyone on screen.

CAPTIONS AND SCREEN COMPOSITION

Do not render any captions, subtitles, logos or other text on screen; accurate synchronized captions are added afterwards. Compose each shot with clear space for them: never place the speaker's face, mouth, important gestures or relevant objects in the caption area. For vertical video, keep faces, mouths and key gestures above about 57% of the frame height (measured from the top) and the region between 57% and 75% free of anything important (captions sit around 60-72%; the bottom 25% and the right edge stay clear for platform buttons). Keep the framing stable so the caption area does not need to move between shots.

FINAL QUALITY CHECK

Check every transition, speaking turn and lip movement. Correct any discontinuity, unnatural gesture, audio drift or inconsistent speech pace before finalizing. End with a brief, natural resting expression, without extra dialogue or exaggerated movements.
