LOOK, CAMERA AND PERFORMANCE: a short rom-com movie, exactly the style of the reference reels (references/reference-study.md)

Picture: a frame from a late-1990s Hollywood romantic comedy shot on 35 mm film: bright, soft and airy, soft diffused daylight, low contrast, natural clean colour that is gently muted (whites stay clean white or cream, skin natural and matte, pastel pinks and creams in the set), fine film grain, a slightly soft picture. Never an orange or amber cast, never a cold grey or teal tint, never saturated, never HDR, never razor-sharp, never a render, CGI, plastic or airbrushed skin. Faces in focus, the background softly out of focus.

Coverage like the reference reels: each line is a single shot of the speaker alone in the frame, a close-up or a medium shot from the waist up, the face large in the upper half of the frame. The speaker talks to the other person, who stands just outside the frame beside the camera: eyes on that person, so the gaze lands just beside the lens, never straight into it and never away from it. A wide shot shows the whole place and both people when something physical happens. Both people share the frame only in a wide or two-shot, standing naturally side by side or across a counter, never a cramped over-the-shoulder angle.

Camera: locked on a tripod, eye level, completely still. ONE CONTINUOUS TAKE, no cuts, no zoom, no pan, no camera movement at all.

Acting: natural, lively and real like good young actors in a rom-com: open expressions, a real grin with teeth, a raised brow, a sly look, a laugh that reaches the eyes; small natural moves of the head and hands. Never stiff, never posed, never a frozen smile, never a glassy stare, never exaggerated or cartoonish. Natural blinks and breathing never stop.

Speech: natural American English at a normal conversational pace, the exact words given, nothing added. Only the speaker's lips move; the jaw opens on every syllable and the lips close when the line ends. Each person keeps one voice throughout. Sound: the voices and the quiet ambience of the place, no music.

Faces: the cast are very young (19-23), beautiful like real young film actors, with real skin texture; the men boyish and handsome. Nobody resembles any real actor, celebrity or public figure. Extras (a baker, a waiter, people on the street) are ordinary people who fill the world naturally.

Vertical 9:16 frame: faces in the upper half; the area three quarters down stays calm because a small subtitle sits there. NO TEXT ON SCREEN: no titles, captions, subtitles, logos or watermarks. LEFT and RIGHT in a face description mean the person's own left and right.
