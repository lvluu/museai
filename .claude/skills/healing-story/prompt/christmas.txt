HEALING CHRISTMAS STORY FOOTAGE REQUIREMENTS

NO TEXT ON SCREEN. Real, unstaged phone-camera footage of an ordinary lived-in city apartment in December, like a calm winter slow-living vlog shot on an iPhone: clear natural exposure, fresh true colours (white walls, pale wood, snow outside, a few deep reds and pine greens), fine sensor grain, real wear and texture.

REAL, NOT AI: it must look filmed, not generated. A plain, CLEAN, tidy kitchen and living room with only a few things in them: scrubbed pale wood, a folded linen tea towel, one cream ceramic bowl, wiped worktops, no clutter, no crumbs or mess. Christmas is quiet and small: at most one small real fir tree with plain warm-white string lights, one simple wreath on the door, one red ribbon; never decorations on every surface, no glitter, no fake snow, no ornaments floating, no rows of identical gifts. Food is handmade and uneven, no two cookies alike, edges a little dark. No orange glow, no HDR look, no glowing halos, no rim light, no bokeh balls, no lens flares. Colours are true: no saturated red-orange wash, no teal-and-orange grade.

LIGHT: one real source per shot. Daytime shots: bright soft white daylight from a window with snow outside, open soft shadows, never murky. Evening shots (only when the shot says evening): the window is deep blue dusk with snow, the room is lit by one plain ceiling or table lamp plus the tree's small warm-white string lights; the room stays clearly visible, never dim or underexposed.

CAMERA: a static locked-off shot, completely still, level horizon. No camera, phone, tripod or filming gear anywhere in the frame. No push-in, no drift, no zoom, no pan; the framing in the last frame is the same as in the first.

LIFE IN THE FRAME (real-time, never a time-lapse): something always moves gently and naturally - snow falling past the window, steam rising from a mug or pot, the hands working, the tree lights steady. Walls, furniture, doors, tableware and window frames stay perfectly rigid and sharp; nothing warps, melts, shimmers or morphs. Objects stay where they are; nothing appears or disappears.

THE GIRL (the narrator; only when the shot mentions her, the SAME woman in every shot): one young woman, about 26, long chestnut-brown hair in the same loose low braid over her left shoulder with a few soft strands at the temples. Her CLOTHES are exactly what the shot names (they change with the day and the year, like a real person's); when the shot names none, a plain soft knit sweater in a muted colour. Never change her hair or face. Her HANDS are beautiful and always the same: slender, smooth, young, soft pale-peach skin, long relaxed fingers, short clean rounded nails with a natural pale-pink polish, no rings, exactly five fingers, natural joints, graceful unhurried movements. Her FACE is always the same (character bank ELLIE): a soft oval face, fair skin with light freckles across the nose and cheeks, light-hazel eyes, natural light-brown brows, no makeup look. Alone she is seen from the side with her eyes on what her hands do; with Mia her face is seen naturally as she talks and laughs. She never looks into the camera.

MIA, HER BEST FRIEND (only when the shot mentions Mia or the friend): one young woman, about 27, a short glossy black bob cut at the jaw. Her CLOTHES are exactly what the shot names (they change with the day and the year); when the shot names none, a dark forest-green chunky knit sweater and a long red wool scarf. Her FACE is always the same (character bank MIA): a round friendly Korean-American face, light warm-tan skin, warm dark-brown almond eyes, straight dark brows, a small soft nose, a small mole under her LEFT eye, a wide easy smile; the bob has a side part on her RIGHT, no fringe. Her hands: slender, light-tan skin, short clean nails, no polish, a thin silver ring on her right index finger, exactly five fingers. Her face is visible whenever she is in the shot; she is warm and lively in a quiet everyday way, but never looks into the camera. Never change her face or hair.

PEOPLE TOGETHER: real friends filmed like a candid home video, framed the way films shoot a conversation: an over-the-shoulder shot, the listener's shoulder and the back of her head soft in the foreground, the speaker's face toward the camera at a slight angle, the two looking at each other, never both facing the camera side by side. Only the speaker's mouth moves, softly and briefly, no words heard; the listener listens, nods once, blinks. Heads, shoulders and hands stay a natural size and in place; faces do not change during the shot. Behaviour is ordinary and human: one person acts, the other reacts a moment later, never both doing the same thing at the same time; small pauses, weight shifts, natural blinks, hands that do something real (holding a mug, a plate, a potato). Warm small smiles and a short chuckle at most; no big open-mouthed laughter, no exaggerated expressions, no frozen smiles, no posing. No hugging close-ups, no crying, no tears. A knock is a whole visible person at the door knocking with a relaxed hand; no close-ups of knocking fists or hands on handles. No knives passed between people.

REACTIONS: people react at real human speed: one change of feeling per shot, a beat of stillness first, a slow soft change of face after it, never a sudden wide-eyed look followed at once by an action. Nobody takes something handed to them the instant they see it; they look, hesitate, then reach.
DOORS: a door is solid and stays: in any shot at a doorway the door leaf stays attached to its frame and visible the whole time, the frame and the wall around it never vanish, melt or turn into open space; a door opens by a person's hand at real speed, never by itself. The person outside stands in a hallway or stairwell, the person inside stands just inside the frame.

SOUND: the real, close-miked sound of exactly what happens in the shot, crisp and in sync, as a phone records it from half a metre away - a knock on a wooden door, a rolling pin on dough, a cookie cutter pressing, a spoon in a mug, soup simmering, a pan sizzling, paper and ribbon, footsteps on stairs, the oven door - over the soft quiet of a snowy city. NO music, no clear words (talking and laughter only as soft room sound), no singing, no humming, no bells or jingles.

Natural lived-in realism, not a cartoon, animation, painting, greeting card, advertisement or video-game render. No watermarks, no subtitles, no logos.
