HEALING FAMILY STORY FOOTAGE REQUIREMENTS

NO TEXT ON SCREEN. Real, unstaged phone-camera footage of ordinary lived-in English homes in late November, like a calm slow-living vlog shot on an iPhone: clear natural exposure, fresh true colours (white and cream walls, pale wood, grey stone, soft greens, soft greys and muted blues), fine sensor grain, real wear and texture. Four places appear, and the shot says which:
- HER FLAT: her small London flat kitchen: white metro tiles, a pale wood worktop, a small white two-ring hob, a white sash window with November rain running down the glass onto wet grey slate rooftops, a small round pale wood table with two chairs, two small pots of herbs on the sill.
- DAD'S KITCHEN: her dad's small old stone cottage kitchen in the Yorkshire Dales: low ceiling with dark wooden beams, cream painted walls, an old cream enamel range cooker, a deep white stone sink under a small window onto the garden, a scrubbed pine table with three plain wooden chairs, a small plain white fridge, a cream corded telephone on the wall by the back door, a worn tartan dog bed by the range.
- DAD'S GARDEN: his small back garden: a small glass greenhouse with tall tomato plants tied to canes, a dry-stone wall, a wet grey stone patio with three stone steps, a weathered wooden bench, green fells and low grey cloud beyond.
- THE CAR: the inside of her small car at night in rain, the wipers moving, wet road and red tail lights ahead.

REAL, NOT AI: it must look filmed, not generated. Plain, CLEAN, tidy rooms with only a few things in them: scrubbed wood, a folded linen tea towel, one mug or one pot, no clutter, no mess. No decorations. Food looks homemade: a dark rich-brown beef stew with orange carrot chunks and pale potato in a dark-red cast-iron pot. No orange glow, no HDR look, no glowing halos, no rim light, no bokeh balls, no lens flares. Colours are true: no saturated red-orange wash, no teal-and-orange grade. Phones are only ever seen lying face down, or with a plain lit screen at a distance; never readable writing, never a name on a screen. Any paper note is seen from a little distance, its handwriting soft and unreadable.

LIGHT: one real source per shot. Daytime: soft bright grey November daylight from the window, open soft shadows, never murky, never golden-hour orange. Early-evening shots (only when the shot says Evening): soft fading blue daylight at the window and one plain ceiling lamp on; the room stays clearly visible, never dim or underexposed. Night car shots: the dashboard's faint light and the road lights through the wet windscreen, her face softly visible.

CAMERA: a static locked-off shot, completely still, level horizon. No camera, tripod or filming gear anywhere in the frame. No push-in, no drift, no zoom, no pan; the framing in the last frame is the same as in the first.

LIFE IN THE FRAME (real-time, never a time-lapse): something always moves gently and naturally - rain running down the window, steam rising from a mug or the pot, leaves moving outside, the hands working, the dog breathing. Walls, furniture, doors, tableware and window frames stay perfectly rigid and sharp; nothing warps, melts, shimmers or morphs. Objects stay where they are; nothing appears or disappears.

THE GIRL (the narrator; only when the shot mentions her, the SAME woman in every shot): one young woman, about 26, long chestnut-brown hair in the same loose low braid over her left shoulder with a few soft strands at the temples. Her CLOTHES are exactly what the shot names (they change with the day and the year, like a real person's); when the shot names none, a plain soft knit sweater in a muted colour. Never change her hair or face. Her HANDS are beautiful and always the same: slender, smooth, young, soft pale-peach skin, long relaxed fingers, short clean rounded nails with a natural pale-pink polish, no rings, exactly five fingers, natural joints, graceful unhurried movements. Her FACE is always the same (character bank ELLIE): a soft oval face, fair skin with light freckles across the nose and cheeks, light-hazel eyes, natural light-brown brows, no makeup look. Alone she is seen from the side with her eyes on what her hands do; with her dad her face is seen naturally as she talks. She never looks into the camera.

RICHARD, HER DAD (only when the shot mentions Dad or Richard): one man, about 60 (character bank RICHARD): a long oval face, fair skin with ruddy cheeks, deep laugh lines, kind light-blue eyes, thick grey brows, short neat grey hair receding a little at the temples, a short trimmed grey beard, plain thin silver wire-framed glasses, a plain gold wedding band on his left ring finger. His CLOTHES are exactly what the shot names; when it names none, a navy wool crew-neck jumper over a light-blue collared shirt. When the shot says sling, his RIGHT forearm is in a plain navy-blue arm sling with a white cast on the wrist, the same in every such shot. He moves slowly and quietly, a man of few words. He never looks into the camera. Never change his face, beard or glasses.

PEPPER, HIS DOG (only when the shot mentions Pepper or the dog; the SAME dog in every shot): an old black-and-white border collie, medium size, a grey muzzle, a narrow white blaze down the middle of the face, a white chest and four white paws, a black body with a slightly thin, soft coat, her LEFT ear folded over and her right ear upright, kind brown eyes. She is old and slow: she lies, lifts her head, gets up stiffly, walks a few slow steps, never runs or jumps.

PEOPLE TOGETHER: father and daughter filmed like a candid home video, framed the way films shoot a conversation: an over-the-shoulder shot, the listener's shoulder and the back of the head soft in the foreground, the speaker's face toward the camera at a slight angle, the two looking at each other, never both facing the camera side by side. Only the speaker's mouth moves, softly and briefly, no words heard; the listener listens, nods once, blinks. Heads, shoulders and hands stay a natural size and in place; faces do not change during the shot. Behaviour is ordinary and human: one person acts, the other reacts a moment later, never both doing the same thing at the same time; small pauses, weight shifts, natural blinks, hands that do something real (holding a mug, a wooden spoon, the dog's head). Warm small smiles that start from something said and fade; never laughing together, no big open-mouthed laughter, no exaggerated expressions, no frozen smiles, no posing. No hugging close-ups, no crying, no tears. No knives anywhere; vegetables are peeled with a plain vegetable peeler.

REACTIONS: people react at real human speed: one change of feeling per shot, a beat of stillness first, a slow soft change of face after it, never a sudden wide-eyed look followed at once by an action.
DOORS: a door is solid and stays: in any shot at a doorway the door leaf stays attached to its frame and visible the whole time, the frame and the wall around it never vanish, melt or turn into open space; a door opens by a person's hand at real speed, never by itself.

SOUND: the real, close-miked sound of exactly what happens in the shot, crisp and in sync, as a phone records it from half a metre away - a phone buzzing on wood, rain on the window, a peeler on a carrot, a wooden spoon in a pot, the stew bubbling softly, a kettle starting to boil, a mug set on wood, the wipers, the dog's claws on stone - over the soft quiet of a home. NO music, no clear words (talking only as soft room sound), no singing, no humming.

Natural lived-in realism, not a cartoon, animation, painting, greeting card, advertisement or video-game render. No watermarks, no subtitles, no logos.
