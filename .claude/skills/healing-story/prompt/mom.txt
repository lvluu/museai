HEALING FAMILY STORY FOOTAGE REQUIREMENTS

NO TEXT ON SCREEN. Real, unstaged phone-camera footage of an ordinary lived-in English cottage in early autumn, like a calm slow-living vlog shot on an iPhone: clear natural exposure, fresh true colours (white and cream walls, pale wood, soft greens, soft greys and muted blues), fine sensor grain, real wear and texture. Three places appear: her small cottage kitchen (white walls, a pale wood worktop, a white butler sink under a window onto the back garden, a small round pale wood table by the window, a small white oven) and its small back garden (raised wooden beds of low strawberry plants, a row of raspberry canes, mint, orange calendula and small purple violas, a gravel path, an old brick wall), and her parents' small red-brick family house (a dark-blue painted front door with a small square window and a brass number 12, a worn stone step, a pot of lavender beside it; inside, a small dining room with cream walls, a rectangular oak table with four plain wooden chairs, a linen-curtained window, one plain pendant lamp over the table). The shot says which. Every fruit, herb and flower on the cake is picked from this garden: small, uneven, real garden berries, never shop-perfect.

REAL, NOT AI: it must look filmed, not generated. A plain, CLEAN, tidy kitchen with only a few things in it: scrubbed pale wood, a folded linen tea towel, one cream ceramic bowl, wiped worktops, no clutter, no crumbs or mess. No party decorations, no party hats, no balloons, no banners, no confetti, no rows of gifts. Cakes are homemade and uneven: a slightly lopsided sponge, cream that is not smooth, jam showing at the edges, real crumbs where it is cut. No orange glow, no HDR look, no glowing halos, no rim light, no bokeh balls, no lens flares. Colours are true: no saturated red-orange wash, no teal-and-orange grade.

LIGHT: one real source per shot. Garden and late-afternoon shots: soft bright natural daylight, open soft shadows, never murky, never golden-hour orange. Kitchen daytime shots: bright soft white daylight from the window. Early-evening shots (only when the shot says early evening): soft fading blue daylight at the window and one plain pendant lamp on; the room stays clearly visible, never dim or underexposed.

CAMERA: a static locked-off shot, completely still, level horizon. No camera, phone, tripod or filming gear anywhere in the frame. No push-in, no drift, no zoom, no pan; the framing in the last frame is the same as in the first.

LIFE IN THE FRAME (real-time, never a time-lapse): something always moves gently and naturally - leaves moving in the garden or outside the window, steam rising from a mug or pot, the hands working. Walls, furniture, doors, tableware and window frames stay perfectly rigid and sharp; nothing warps, melts, shimmers or morphs. Objects stay where they are; nothing appears or disappears.

THE GIRL (the narrator; only when the shot mentions her, the SAME woman in every shot): one young woman, about 26, long chestnut-brown hair in the same loose low braid over her left shoulder with a few soft strands at the temples. Her CLOTHES are exactly what the shot names (they change with the day and the year, like a real person's); when the shot names none, a plain soft knit sweater in a muted colour. Never change her hair or face. Her HANDS are beautiful and always the same: slender, smooth, young, soft pale-peach skin, long relaxed fingers, short clean rounded nails with a natural pale-pink polish, no rings, exactly five fingers, natural joints, graceful unhurried movements. Her FACE is always the same (character bank ELLIE): a soft oval face, fair skin with light freckles across the nose and cheeks, light-hazel eyes, natural light-brown brows, no makeup look. Alone she is seen from the side with her eyes on what her hands do; with her mother her face is seen naturally as she talks. She never looks into the camera.

ANNE, HER MOTHER (only when the shot mentions Anne or her mother): one woman, about 57, slight build. Her CLOTHES are exactly what the shot names (they change with the day and the year); when the shot names none, a soft oatmeal cotton cardigan over a pale-blue cotton blouse. Her FACE is always the same (character bank ANNE): a soft oval face, fair skin with faded freckles across the nose, fine laugh lines at the corners of her eyes, soft lines beside the mouth, light-hazel eyes, thin light-brown brows, thin natural lips, no makeup look; chin-length soft light-brown hair streaked with grey, a side part on her LEFT, tucked behind her RIGHT ear; small plain gold stud earrings. Her HANDS: a real 57-year-old's hands, slender with soft fine lines and a few faint freckles, short clean unpolished nails, a thin gold wedding band on her left ring finger and no other rings, exactly five fingers. She moves quietly and practically, the way someone who has done this a thousand times does. She never looks into the camera. Never change her face or hair.

RICHARD, HER DAD (only when the shot mentions Dad or Richard): one man, about 60 (character bank RICHARD): a long oval face, fair skin with ruddy cheeks, deep laugh lines, kind light-blue eyes, thick grey brows, short neat grey hair receding a little at the temples, a short trimmed grey beard, plain thin silver wire-framed glasses, a plain gold wedding band. His CLOTHES are exactly what the shot names; when it names none, a navy wool crew-neck jumper over a light-blue collared shirt. Never change his face, beard or glasses.

TOM, HER BROTHER (only when the shot mentions Tom or her brother): one man, about 30 (character bank TOM): a squarish face, fair skin, light dark-brown stubble, warm brown eyes, straight dark-brown brows with a small pale scar through his LEFT eyebrow, short dark-brown hair cropped at the sides and a little tousled on top. His CLOTHES are exactly what the shot names; when it names none, an olive-green cotton crew-neck sweatshirt. Never change his face or hair.

THE CAKE (whenever the shot shows the birthday cake, it is always this one cake, unchanged from shot to shot): a two-layer homemade Victoria sponge on a plain white round plate, a little lopsided, red raspberry jam and white whipped cream showing between the two pale-golden layers, rough white whipped cream on top, a ring of small halved red garden strawberries around the top edge, a few whole raspberries in the middle, three small purple violas, one orange calendula flower and two small mint leaves. Once the shot says candles, five thin plain white candles stand in the cream, each with one small steady flame. Never a different cake, never extra tiers, icing, writing or decorations.

PEOPLE TOGETHER: a real family (mother and daughter, or the four of them) filmed like a candid home video, framed the way films shoot a conversation: an over-the-shoulder shot, the listener's shoulder and the back of her head soft in the foreground, the speaker's face toward the camera at a slight angle, the two looking at each other, never both facing the camera side by side. The family at the table is shot like a real family dinner on a phone: seated around the oak table at natural angles, some faces turned toward each other, never lined up facing the camera. Only the speaker's mouth moves, softly and briefly, no words heard; the listener listens, nods once, blinks. Heads, shoulders and hands stay a natural size and in place; faces do not change during the shot. Behaviour is ordinary and human: one person acts, the other reacts a moment later, never both doing the same thing at the same time; small pauses, weight shifts, natural blinks, hands that do something real (holding a mug, a plate, a spatula). Warm small smiles and short natural laughs that start from something said and fade; never everyone laughing at the same moment, no big open-mouthed laughter, no exaggerated expressions, no frozen smiles, no posing. No hugging close-ups, no crying, no tears. A knock is a whole visible person at the door knocking with a relaxed hand; no close-ups of knocking fists or hands on handles. No knives anywhere; a cake is cut with a flat cake server.

REACTIONS: people react at real human speed: one change of feeling per shot, a beat of stillness first, a slow soft change of face after it, never a sudden wide-eyed look followed at once by an action. Nobody takes something handed to them the instant they see it; they look, hesitate, then reach.
DOORS: a door is solid and stays: in any shot at a doorway the door leaf stays attached to its frame and visible the whole time, the frame and the wall around it never vanish, melt or turn into open space; a door opens by a person's hand at real speed, never by itself. The person outside stands in a hallway, stairwell or on a front step, the person inside stands just inside the frame.

SOUND: the real, close-miked sound of exactly what happens in the shot, crisp and in sync, as a phone records it from half a metre away - flour through a sieve, a wooden spoon in a bowl, a whisk in cream, a spatula on a cake, a cake tin on a rack, a tap running, a plate set on wood, a knock on a wooden door, footsteps, a spoon in a mug - over the soft quiet of a home. NO music, no clear words (talking only as soft room sound), no singing voices, no humming; when the shot says the family sings, their mouths move gently together but the sound stays soft room tone.

Natural lived-in realism, not a cartoon, animation, painting, greeting card, advertisement or video-game render. No watermarks, no subtitles, no logos.
