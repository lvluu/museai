HEALING FRIEND STORY FOOTAGE REQUIREMENTS

NO TEXT ON SCREEN. Real, unstaged phone-camera footage of an ordinary lived-in small terraced house in an English town in mid-autumn, like a calm slow-living vlog shot on an iPhone: clear natural exposure, fresh true colours (white walls, pale wood, soft greens, soft greys, warm autumn reds and oranges outside), fine sensor grain, real wear and texture. Three places appear: Nora's small kitchen (white walls, a pale wood worktop, a white sink under a window onto a small back yard with a young maple tree in red-orange autumn leaves, a small white oven and hob, a small round pale-wood table with three plain wooden chairs by the window, a white fridge with two child's crayon drawings held by magnets), Poppy's small bedroom (white walls, a single bed with a pale-yellow duvet, a small white bedside table, one low shelf of picture books), and the front of the house (a sage-green painted front door with a small brass letterbox, a worn stone step, a pot of orange chrysanthemums beside it). The shot says which.

AUTUMN AT HOME: the kitchen always carries the season, plain and lived-in: a bowl of red apples on the table, two small orange pumpkins on the windowsill, red-orange maple leaves moving in the yard outside. Never a decor showroom, never Halloween decorations.

REAL, NOT AI: it must look filmed, not generated. A plain, CLEAN, tidy kitchen with only a few things in it: scrubbed pale wood, a folded linen tea towel, one cream ceramic bowl, wiped worktops, no clutter. Food is homemade and uneven: apple slices of different sizes, a lumpy crumble topping, golden-brown with dark edges where it bubbled. No orange glow, no HDR look, no glowing halos, no rim light, no bokeh balls, no lens flares. Colours are true: no saturated red-orange wash, no teal-and-orange grade.

LIGHT: one real source per shot. Daytime shots: bright soft white autumn daylight from the window, open soft shadows, never murky, never golden-hour orange. Evening shots (only when the shot says Evening): soft blue dusk at the window and one plain ceiling light on; the room stays clearly visible, never dim or underexposed.

CAMERA: a static locked-off shot, completely still, level horizon. No camera, phone, tripod or filming gear anywhere in the frame. No push-in, no drift, no zoom, no pan; the framing in the last frame is the same as in the first. The camera never follows a person.

LIFE IN THE FRAME (real-time, never a time-lapse): something always moves gently and naturally - maple leaves moving outside the window, steam rising from a mug or pan, the hands working. Walls, furniture, doors, tableware and window frames stay perfectly rigid and sharp; nothing warps, melts, shimmers or morphs. Objects stay where they are; nothing appears or disappears.

ELLIE (the narrator, Nora's best friend; only when the shot mentions Ellie, the SAME woman in every shot, character bank ELLIE): one young woman, about 27, long chestnut-brown hair in the same loose low braid over her LEFT shoulder with a few soft strands at the temples, a soft oval face, fair skin with light freckles across the nose and cheeks, light-hazel eyes, natural light-brown brows, no makeup look, no rings. Dark-brown wavy hair to just below the shoulders, a side part on her LEFT, tucked behind her RIGHT ear; small plain gold hoop earrings; no rings. Small child's hands, exactly five fingers. Her CLOTHES are exactly what the shot names. She moves like a real child: careful with concentration, a little slow, serious when she works. She never looks into the camera.

NO OTHER ADULTS: the man in the story is never shown, not his face, hands or back.

PEOPLE TOGETHER: filmed like a candid home video, framed the way films shoot a conversation: an over-the-shoulder shot, the listener's shoulder and the back of her head soft in the foreground, the speaker's face toward the camera at a slight angle, the two looking at each other, never both facing the camera side by side. Only the speaker's mouth moves, softly and briefly, no words heard; the listener listens, nods once, blinks. Faces do not change during the shot. Behaviour is ordinary and human: one person acts, the other reacts a moment later, never both doing the same thing at the same time; small pauses, natural blinks, hands that do something real. Warm small smiles that start from something said and fade; never two people laughing together, no open-mouthed laughter, no exaggerated expressions, no frozen smiles, no posing. No hugging close-ups, no crying, no tears. No knives anywhere; apples are peeled with a plain vegetable peeler.

REACTIONS: people react at real human speed: one change of feeling per shot, a beat of stillness first, a slow soft change of face after it. Nobody takes something handed to them the instant they see it; they look, pause, then reach.
DOORS: a door is solid and stays: in any shot at a doorway the door leaf stays attached to its frame and visible the whole time, the frame and the wall around it never vanish, melt or turn into open space; a door opens by a person's hand at real speed, never by itself. The person outside stands on the front step, the person inside stands just inside the frame.

SOUND: the real, close-miked sound of exactly what happens in the shot, crisp and in sync, as a phone records it from half a metre away - tea pouring into a mug, a peeler on an apple, flour into a bowl, a wooden spoon in a pan, apples softly bubbling, a dish on a rack, a spoon on a bowl, a page turning, crayons on paper, footsteps on a wooden floor - over the soft quiet of a home and a little wind outside. NO music, no clear words (talking only as soft room sound), no singing voices, no humming sound; when the shot says she hums, her lips move gently but the sound stays soft room tone.

Natural lived-in realism, not a cartoon, animation, painting, greeting card, advertisement or video-game render. No watermarks, no subtitles, no logos.
